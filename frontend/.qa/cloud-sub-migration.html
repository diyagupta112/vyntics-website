Signs it is time to plan your cloud move

An on-premise to cloud migration should address a business or operating constraint, not just move the same problems elsewhere.

Aging servers

Hardware refreshes, unsupported software, and limited spare capacity make routine changes harder to manage.

Scaling limits

Demand changes faster than your infrastructure can respond, or capacity planning depends on large purchases made well in advance.

High maintenance costs

Your team spends time patching, replacing hardware, and maintaining environments that could be simpler to operate.

Security gaps

Access controls, patching, backups, or recovery procedures need a more consistent approach before the next major change.

Our cloud architecture services

Our cloud architecture consulting starts with your workloads, dependencies, and ownership needs. We document the choices your team and cloud solution architect need to review.

Well-architected design

We map application boundaries, traffic, data flows, and service requirements before choosing compute, storage, and managed services.

Landing zones

We define the account or subscription structure, environment separation, identity controls, and policies that support consistent deployment.

Security and network design

We plan private connectivity, access boundaries, encryption, and network segmentation around the systems your workloads need to reach.

High availability and disaster recovery

We design redundancy, backup, and recovery options against agreed business requirements, then identify what needs to be tested.

Our cloud migration services

We select a migration approach for each workload. The right choice depends on application constraints, risk, and the changes your team is ready to own.

Rehost: lift and shift

Move a workload with limited application changes when a straightforward infrastructure move is appropriate.

Replatform

Adjust selected platform components, such as a database or application runtime, while keeping most application behavior intact.

Refactor

Change application architecture where the business case supports the additional development, testing, and operational work.

Database and application migration

Plan data transfer, dependency sequencing, validation, cutover, and rollback together so applications and their data move coherently.

Frequently asked questions

Practical answers about cloud architecture & migration.

Have a question about your environment? Contact our team.

How long does a cloud migration take?

A migration typically moves through discovery, planning, testing, and staged cutover. The schedule depends on workload count, data volume, dependencies, testing needs, and available change windows. We assess those factors before proposing a delivery plan.

How much does cloud migration cost?

Costs typically depend on assessment effort, application changes, data transfer, testing, and the target cloud services. Running old and new environments together can also affect the budget during the move. We separate delivery scope from ongoing cloud operating costs when discussing an engagement.

Will there be downtime during migration?

Some workloads need a cutover window, while others can support a staged transition. The practical approach depends on data consistency, application design, and the systems connected to it. We plan the change window and rollback steps rather than promising a migration without downtime.

How do you keep our data safe during migration?

We plan access controls, encrypted transfer, backups, and validation around your data requirements. Permissions should be limited to the people and systems involved in the move. We agree on checks for data integrity and recovery before cutover.

What is the difference between rehosting, replatforming and refactoring?

Rehosting moves a workload with limited application changes. Replatforming changes selected platform components while keeping much of the application intact. Refactoring changes the application architecture and typically requires more development and testing.

Should we choose AWS, Azure or Google Cloud?

The choice depends on your applications, existing systems, skills, security requirements, and cost model. There is no single best platform for every workload. We compare suitable options against the requirements you need to meet.
